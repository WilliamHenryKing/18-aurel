# AUREL design direction

## The feeling

Nocturnal, intimate, precise. AUREL is a place to slow down and notice the relationship between light, a surface and the way a room feels. Its audience is a visitor considering architecture, interiors or finishing, not a software buyer. The fictional nature of the work stays clear without overwhelming the experience.

## Visual grammar

Ink and charcoal form the page: `#141614`, `#222621`. Warm ivory `#e9e6de` carries the typography and aged brass `#c4ae83` provides a controlled accent. One pale olive/ivory invitation section changes the rhythm near the end. Images use generous scale; deliberately uneven project proportions resist a repeated card grid on the home page.

Cormorant Garamond supplies very large, delicate editorial headings with genuine italic cuts. Local Manrope is used for quiet metadata, navigation and body text. Type is part of the architecture of the composition: the hero's second line is indented, the invitation spans most of the page, and interior pages carry substantial opening statements.

The oversized hero and invitation are retained from the committed brief as an explicit exception to Impeccable's generic 6rem display ceiling. Tracking never tightens beyond -0.04em. Body text starts at 16px and metadata at 14px. Headings stand on their own, without kickers or decorative section numbers. All icons are original stroke SVG; emoji and Unicode icon substitutions are prohibited.

The original generated residence at dusk is the opening world. The original travertine stair hall extends that world. The finishing section gives a 16:10 architectural render gallery most of its width: The space shows a walnut-lined room opening into a garden; The detail concentrates on travertine, timber flutes and bronze joinery. A restrained caption and native view buttons sit below, with the copy above so the architecture carries the section. The final CUDA assets are pending delivery and review; their wired filenames are `aurel-world(-mobile).webp` and `aurel-timber-study(-mobile).webp`. No invented client names, awards, testimonials, certification or completed-building claims are permitted. The four projects are openly described as speculative studies; the remaining bedroom photograph is a licensed atmosphere reference, never claimed as AUREL's design.

## Motion and spatial interaction

GSAP `useGSAP` scopes and reverts a restrained hero image settle and the material gallery's ink aperture, image settle and caption fade. The later user request adds one sustained cinematic moment inside the interactive pavilion: the camera approaches the threshold, moves towards the timber screen and daybed, then travels laterally to settle beside the water. Paired camera and look-target curves make the change of subject deliberate. The remaining editorial sections are immediately readable, without repeated rising entrances. The default CSS image is visible; reduced motion skips choreography. Native scrolling remains intact and a visible footer control lets the visitor stop motion. No cursor replacement, scroll hijack, perpetual WebGL animation or loading screen is used.

The interactive light pavilion is a composed architectural section: floor, stepped plinth, perimeter stone walls, split roof and rooflight, fine timber screen, restrained bronze structure, built-in bench, daybed, small vessel and reflecting basin. It remains explicitly separate from the offline rendered residence. Daylight reveals the material surfaces; dusk warms the interior and deepens the shell. Phone camera positions pull back along the same sightlines, with closer views intentionally concentrating on one part of the model.

The labelled GSAP timeline drives Catmull–Rom camera and target paths through Threshold, Timber screen and Water, with a short hold at the screen and at the final composition. ScrollTrigger uses a 0.55-second scrub against one CSS sticky stage, without pinning or intercepting input. The travel is 115svh on desktop and 88svh at widths up to 700px. The three native chapter buttons can move directly to a composition through ScrollToPlugin; an explicit Material controls button skips the sequence and focuses the first lighting control. Captions and the progress line explain the current subject without overlaying large text on the model.

Pause camera freezes the automatic path. Hour, finish and perspective controls give the visitor ownership until Resume scroll or a chapter jump; later scrolling cannot silently override that choice. Subtle mouse elevation is available only after a perspective button is used. Reduced motion and the global motion-off setting preserve immediate named-view selection without the scroll runway, and switching motion off mid-passage holds the current composition. Viewports shorter than 600px also use the compact non-sticky presentation. Timelines and triggers are owned by scoped `useGSAP`/`matchMedia` cleanup; chapter and skip scroll tweens deliberately live outside that context, because reverting a context re-renders recorded tweens at their start and would move the page. GSAP alone smooths programmatic scrolling: CSS `scroll-behavior: smooth` made ScrollToPlugin's auto-kill abandon chapter jumps and is not used.

The sticky stage is at most 980px tall, so large screens are filled. Wide canvases narrow the vertical field of view (above a 2.1 aspect) so the section keeps its scale instead of opening a horizontal view past 90 degrees. Short desktop screens use lighter stage chrome. Chapter jumps are announced politely; the perspective arrows stay focusable at their limits. A lost WebGL context shows the drawn fallback and a restored one rebuilds the reflection map and resumes. A failed late chunk shows the placeholder instead of blanking the page.

The below-fold scene now has an IntersectionObserver boundary 400px ahead of its container. The Three.js scene module is requested only when that boundary is reached or the visitor navigates directly to `#/light`. A reserved architectural placeholder holds its place. Once mounted, the existing scene visibility, idle rendering, DPR caps, fallback and resource cleanup continue to apply.

The official GreenSock development references are pinned to [gsap-skills commit aed9cfd3277740755f6bfc1155c7aa645403b760](https://github.com/greensock/gsap-skills/tree/aed9cfd3277740755f6bfc1155c7aa645403b760). Root verified seven installed skills: core, timeline, ScrollTrigger, plugins, utils, React and performance. The six relevant camera skills were read in full for this implementation. User-authorised Impeccable hooks may operate, but this design note does not claim automatic execution. The manual detector was rerun once on 4 October after the camera and accessibility corrections; it reports only the documented geometric fallback exception (`docs/IMPECCABLE-DETECTOR.md`).

## Responsive composition

Desktop uses wide margins and long views; tablet collapses the supporting editorial columns; phone turns the project sequence into an intentionally offset image journal. The header changes to an expandable navigation below 680px. All real controls remain available at 320px with at least 44px targets; the pavilion controls stack at the narrowest width. The material close-up keeps its 16:10 composition and extends to the phone's edges. The enquiry page never requires personal information.

## Acceptance boundary

Source/type/lint checks establish implementation health. The camera source passed typecheck and scoped Biome checks; final renders, integrated build, camera/browser review and live verification remain pending. First-release receipts and captures retain their historical scope. Actual browser captures, interaction, keyboard, reduced-motion, image loading, WebGL fallback, console/network and responsive checks establish local experience evidence. They do not establish real clients, completed architecture or user acceptance. Root owns the shared visual/browser review and authorised release, with render provenance and reproduction instructions to accompany the final package in `docs/RENDERING.md`.
